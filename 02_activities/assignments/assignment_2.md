Good: https://datavizproject.com/data-type/population-pyramid-2/
Aesthetic

The visualization is aesthetically effective because it uses a simple and balanced design that immediately draws attention to the data. The contrasting blue and red colors clearly distinguish males from females while remaining visually harmonious. The graph avoids unnecessary decorative elements, allowing the viewer to focus on the population distribution itself. The age groups are arranged in a clean vertical hierarchy, and the labels are large enough to be read easily without cluttering the figure. The mirrored structure of the population pyramid also creates visual symmetry, making the chart appear organized and professional. Overall, the combination of color contrast, spacing, and clear labeling improves readability and helps viewers interpret the information quickly.

Substantive

The visualization effectively presents a large amount of demographic information in a compact format. The vertical axis divides the population into five-year age intervals, allowing viewers to examine changes across different stages of life. The horizontal axis displays the percentage of the total population rather than raw counts, making comparisons between age groups more meaningful. By placing males on the left and females on the right, the graph allows direct comparison between genders within the same age category. Population pyramids are specifically designed to communicate age and sex distributions, making this chart type highly appropriate for the data being presented. The visualization therefore succeeds in conveying the demographic structure of Denmark in 2015 in a way that is both informative and efficient.

Perceptual

The primary message of the visualization can be understood almost immediately. The widths of the bars make it easy to compare the relative size of different age groups, while the mirrored layout allows viewers to identify similarities and differences between males and females. The chart reveals that Denmark has a relatively mature population structure, with several middle-aged and older age groups being comparable in size to younger groups. Unlike a rapidly growing population, the pyramid does not have a very broad base, suggesting lower birth rates and an aging population. The narrowing bars among the oldest age groups also clearly illustrate the decline in population at advanced ages. Because the visual encoding relies on length and position, which people perceive accurately, viewers can quickly identify trends and draw meaningful conclusions without extensive explanation.

Bad: https://viz.wtf/ （Time dilation, fourth visualization from the top)
Aesthetic

Although the visualization is visually appealing and immediately captures the viewer's attention, its artistic design comes at the expense of readability. The spiral shape is unusual and creates visual interest, which is likely why it was chosen by The New York Times. However, the overlapping curves, varying band widths, and circular layout make the graphic appear cluttered. The viewer must spend additional time understanding how to read the chart before interpreting the data itself. Furthermore, the large amount of empty space in the center contributes little informational value while reducing the area available for displaying the data. In this case, the design prioritizes visual novelty over simplicity and efficiency.

Substantive

The visualization attempts to show the progression of COVID-19 cases in the United States across 2020, 2021, and early 2022 while highlighting seasonal patterns. However, the spiral format makes it difficult to extract precise information from the chart. Unlike a traditional line graph with clearly labeled axes, viewers cannot easily determine the exact number of cases at a given point in time. The radial distance from the center represents case counts, but interpreting differences in radial length is less intuitive than comparing heights on a standard vertical axis. Additionally, because each year occupies a different position on the spiral, direct quantitative comparisons between years become challenging. The chart successfully conveys that case counts increased dramatically over time, but it does not support accurate numerical analysis.

Perceptual

From a perceptual standpoint, the visualization violates several principles of effective data communication. Humans are generally better at comparing lengths along a common scale than comparing distances measured from a central point. In this chart, viewers must estimate values based on radial position and curved paths, which increases cognitive effort. The spiral also distorts the perception of growth because larger values are displayed farther from the center, making increases appear more dramatic due to the expanding circumference. As a result, the visualization may exaggerate the apparent magnitude of later COVID-19 waves. The seasonal alignment of months around the circle does help reveal recurring surges during similar times of the year, but this benefit is outweighed by the difficulty of accurately comparing case levels across different periods. A standard time-series line graph would communicate the same information more clearly and allow viewers to identify trends, peaks, and comparisons with much less effort.

Improvements for the Population Pyramid Visualization

Although the population pyramid is already an effective visualization, several improvements could further enhance its usefulness. First, the graph could include exact percentage values or interactive labels for each age group, allowing viewers to make more precise comparisons without estimating bar lengths. Additional contextual information, such as the total population size, median age, or population growth rate, could also provide a deeper understanding of Denmark's demographic structure. Furthermore, the use of color could be optimized to improve accessibility for individuals with color vision deficiencies by incorporating patterns or shades that remain distinguishable in grayscale. Finally, annotations highlighting notable demographic features, such as the aging population or unusually large age cohorts, would help guide viewers toward the most important insights without requiring them to independently interpret the trends.

Improvements for the COVID-19 Spiral Visualization

The COVID-19 spiral visualization could be significantly improved by prioritizing clarity and quantitative accuracy over visual novelty. One improvement would be to include a conventional radial scale with clearly labeled tick marks, making it easier for viewers to estimate the number of cases represented at different points on the spiral. The chart could also benefit from stronger annotations that identify major COVID-19 waves, variants, or significant public health events, helping viewers understand the reasons behind changes in case counts. Most importantly, the spiral layout could be replaced with, or supplemented by, a standard line graph that uses a common horizontal time axis and vertical case-count axis. Such a design would allow viewers to compare case levels across different years more accurately and reduce the cognitive effort required to interpret the data. While the spiral effectively emphasizes the cyclical nature of the pandemic, a simpler representation would communicate trends and magnitudes more clearly and support more accurate analysis.
